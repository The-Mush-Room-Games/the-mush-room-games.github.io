# Spore Nocturne

A forest seen only by its own light. Space is a black room with depth, never a flat backdrop: the far forest is a silhouette cut from the dark, the middle distance glows, and the ground sits close enough to touch. Each layer moves at its own speed, so the eye reads distance from motion alone. Nothing explains itself; the scene just keeps going.

Every pixel sits on one grid. No sprite is enlarged, shrunk or smoothed relative to another, because a single stray scale breaks the spell. The work should feel meticulously crafted: each placement chosen by hand, every overlap considered, and no accidental fragments left from a sheet. It should look like hours of patient, master-level placement, not something assembled.

Colour is a small, strict set of ramps: violet for distance, cyan and pink for what is lit, acid lime only where light catches the ground. Each sprite is remapped onto one ramp by its own light and shadow, so borrowed art becomes one family. Black does most of the work. Light comes from one place, low and warm-pink, like a stage just out of frame, and every lit edge agrees with it.

Rhythm comes from repetition with drift: tall forms spaced unevenly, small forms clustered in threes and twos, gaps that let the dark breathe. The loop has no seam. The tile must be painstakingly balanced so the eye never catches the moment it repeats.

Text has no place inside the scene. One moving figure is the only story, and everything else is the room it moves through. Restraint is the craft: no glow on everything, no clutter, only depth, rhythm and one light, executed with the care of someone at the top of their field.
